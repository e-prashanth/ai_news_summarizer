## Highlights

### OpenAI Safety Employee Resigns Over Company Culture
David Robinson, an OpenAI safety employee who previously wrote safety reports for major model releases, has resigned from the company. Following his departure, Robinson published an editorial in *The Atlantic* claiming that OpenAI's culture is broken and warning about AI risks.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken/)

### Apple Limits Mac Full-Disk Access Due to AI Agent Risks
Apple is rolling out new permissions controls to restrict full-disk access on Mac computers. The company stated that the limits are designed to prevent potential abuse as AI agents substantially increase security risks associated with high-level system access.
Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1004295/apple-limit-mac-disk-access-ai-agents)

### Meta Open-Sources Code for Custom Muse AI Gadgets
Meta has open-sourced code allowing developers to build custom hardware devices powered by its Muse AI agent, suggesting uses such as color E Ink displays or HDMI sticks. The release comes as millions have downloaded the Muse agent, which creates detailed profiles of users' friends and family.
Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1004330/meta-muse-ai-gadgets-home-link)

### Sean Parker Rebuilds Stability AI Around Music
Sean Parker is restructuring Stability AI to focus specifically on music applications. The initiative is supported by funding and backing from major record labels.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/sean-parker-is-rebuilding-stability-ai-around-music/)

### Capcom Outlines AI Integration Strategy for Game Development
During the Capcom Open Conference RE: 2026, programmer Satoshi Ishida presented updates on the REX Project, an evolution of Capcom's RE Engine. The studio indicated it is preparing for a future where it builds video games in collaboration with AI.
Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1004418/capcom-ai-game-development)

### Nvidia Shield TV Price Increases $100 Amid AI Memory Demand
Nvidia raised the price of its seven-year-old Shield TV streaming device by $100. The price increase comes as memory costs surge across the hardware market due to demand driven by AI technology.
